\textbf{Question.} A finite experiment ends at some length or time scale $R$.
When does a relevant process exist beyond $R$? There are two distinct answers.
A point-process model can make further scales probable by assumption. A fluid
equation can provide a mechanism that transfers activity toward larger scales.
Neither answer lets a finite record prove that nature continues forever.

\section*{1. A probability law for further scales}
Index relevant processes by a characteristic scale $L\ge L_{\min}$. Equal
multiplicative steps in $L$ are equal intervals in $\log L$. Assume that
relevant scales form a Poisson point process with intensity
\[
  d\mu(L)=\eta(L)\frac{dL}{L},\qquad \eta(L)\ge0.
\]
This is a population assumption: ``relevant'' means that a counted process is
dynamically coupled to the phenomenon, not merely present elsewhere. If
$V(L)=v_dL^d$, then $dV/V=d\,dL/L$; that dimensional factor is absorbed into
$\eta$, since finite counts identify only the product.

The number $N(R,S)$ of processes with scales in $(R,S]$ is Poisson with mean
\[
  \mu(R,S)=\int_R^S\eta(L)\frac{dL}{L}.
\]
Hence its zero-count probability gives
\[
 \Prob\{N(R,S)\ge1\}
 =1-\exp\!\left[-\int_R^S\eta(L)\frac{dL}{L}\right].                   \tag{1}
\]
Letting $S\to\infty$ yields the sharp criterion
\[
 \boxed{\text{unbounded process scales almost surely}}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \boxed{\displaystyle\int_R^\infty\eta(L)\frac{dL}{L}=\infty}.       \tag{2}
\]

\textbf{Coin-flip intuition.} If $\eta(L)=\eta_0>0$, each doubling band
$(2^jR,2^{j+1}R]$ has the same hit probability
\[
 p=1-\exp(-\eta_0\log2)=1-2^{-\eta_0}.
\]
The bands are independent. The probability of missing the first $m$ bands is
$2^{-m\eta_0}\to0$, just as repeated fair coin flips eventually produce
heads. In fact, infinitely many bands contain a process almost surely. This is
the scale-invariant Poisson process studied by Arratia~\cite{arratia}.

The opposite conclusion is equally possible. If
$\eta(L)=\eta_0(L_{\min}/L)^a$ with $a>0$, then
\[
 \int_R^\infty\eta(L)\frac{dL}{L}
 =\frac{\eta_0}{a}\left(\frac{L_{\min}}R\right)^a<\infty.
\]
There is consequently a positive probability
\[
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{\eta_0}{a}
 \left(\frac{L_{\min}}R\right)^a\right]
\]
that no relevant process exists beyond $R$.

\textbf{Why finite data cannot decide.} Choose two intensity functions that
agree for every observed $L\le S_{\rm obs}$, but give one a constant tail and
the other a power-decaying tail. They induce exactly the same observations
inside the measured range, while (2) gives opposite answers outside it. The
continuation claim therefore comes from the tail model, not from the finite
record.

\section*{2. What Navier--Stokes can supply}
Two-dimensional turbulence supplies a physical route to larger scales. Consider
the stochastically forced, damped Navier--Stokes equations on a periodic box
$\mathbb T_\lambda^2$:
\[
 du+\big[(u\!\cdot\!\nabla)u+\nabla p\big]dt
 =\big[\nu\Delta u-\alpha(-\Delta)^{-2\gamma}u\big]dt+dW^\lambda,
 \qquad \nabla\!\cdot u=0.                                             \tag{3}
\]
The forcing acts near a fixed injection scale and supplies mean energy
$\varepsilon>0$ per unit time and area. Bedrossian, Coti Zelati,
Punshon-Smith, and Weber proved rigorous inverse-flux laws for stationary
solutions in the large-box limit~\cite{bcpw}. Their hypotheses include regular
forcing, $\lambda\to\infty$, and weak anomalous dissipation as
$\nu,\alpha\to0$:
\[
\nu\E\|\omega\|_2^2\to0,
 \qquad
 \alpha\E\|(-\Delta)^{-\gamma}\omega\|_2^2\to0,
 \qquad \omega=\operatorname{curl}u.                                  \tag{4}
\]
Here the exponent $-2\gamma$ in the velocity equation becomes $-\gamma$ in
the quadratic dissipation norm; this is the convention of Ref.~\cite{bcpw}.

For the velocity increment $\delta_{\ell n}u(x)=u(x+\ell n)-u(x)$, define
\[
 \mathcal F_\ell(u)=
 \avgint_{S^1}\avgint_{\mathbb T_\lambda^2}
 |\delta_{\ell n}u|^2(\delta_{\ell n}u\cdot n)\,dx\,dn.
\]
There is an outer inertial scale $\ell_\alpha\to\infty$ such that
\[
 \E\mathcal F_\ell(u)\sim2\varepsilon\ell,
 \qquad 1\ll\ell\ll\ell_\alpha.                                    \tag{5}
\]
The positive sign is the inverse cascade: energy crosses toward larger
lengths. Thus, for every finite $R$, sufficiently large boxes and sufficiently
weak drag admit an active inverse-flux scale $\ell\ge R$.

For each fixed box, viscosity, and positive drag, suppose additionally that the
chosen invariant measure is ergodic. Birkhoff's theorem then gives
\[
 \frac1T\int_0^T\mathcal F_\ell(u(t))\,dt
 \longrightarrow \E\mathcal F_\ell(u)>0.                              \tag{6}
\]
Thus positive inverse transfer recurs at each admitted scale almost surely.
This stationary result concerns arbitrarily large scales only across a
large-box, vanishing-drag family; a fixed box still has a largest scale.

\section*{3. The stronger theorem still wanted}
The desired single-flow statement would construct a homogeneous statistical
solution on $\mathbb R^2$, with finite energy per unit area (not a finite-total-
energy $L^2(\mathbb R^2)$ solution). Let $E(k,t)$ be its isotropic energy
spectrum and, for fixed $\theta\in(0,1)$, define the infrared energy-quantile
length
\[
 k_\theta(t)=\inf\!\left\{K>0:\int_0^K E(k,t)\,dk\ge
 \theta\int_0^\infty E(k,t)\,dk\right\},\qquad L(t)=k_\theta(t)^{-1}.
\]
The target is then the well-posed growth statement
\[
 \Prob\{L(t)\to\infty\}=1.                                             \tag{7}
\]
The familiar rate follows if one additionally assumes the
Kraichnan inverse-cascade spectrum~\cite{kraichnan}
\[
 E(k)=C_I\varepsilon^{2/3}k^{-5/3},
 \qquad L(t)^{-1}\le k\le L_f^{-1}.
\]
Indeed, equating accumulated energy $\varepsilon t$ with the spectral integral
gives
\[
 \varepsilon t
 =\frac{3C_I}{2}\varepsilon^{2/3}
   \big(L(t)^{2/3}-L_f^{2/3}\big),
\]
so
\[
 \boxed{L(t)=\left[L_f^{2/3}+
 \frac{2}{3C_I}\varepsilon^{1/3}t\right]^{3/2}}
 \sim \left(\frac{2}{3C_I}\right)^{3/2}\varepsilon^{1/2}t^{3/2}.       \tag{8}
\]
Equation (8) is still a closure calculation, not a theorem derived from (3).
Here $L$ is an infrared-front proxy (different fixed $\theta$ change constants,
not the predicted exponent). This evolving regime is nonstationary, so the
fixed-parameter Birkhoff argument in (6) does not imply (7) or (8). The missing
step is to prove an expanding infrared front or the $k^{-5/3}$ spectrum directly
from Navier--Stokes on an unbounded domain; homogeneous forcing makes this
harder because total energy is infinite. Large-scale drag, finite forcing
duration, or a finite container can stop the growth. The research target is to
establish (7), with explicit hypotheses and cutoffs, without assuming the
spectrum that already implies it.
